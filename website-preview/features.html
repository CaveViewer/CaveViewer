CaveViewer features

Render What Others Can’t

Huge cave scans often exceed the practical limits of a conventional viewer, leaving people waiting for a model to load or forced to simplify it. CaveViewer builds a reusable spatial cache and loads nearby chunks as you move, keeping a large map responsive without holding the whole model in memory at once. It lets teams inspect a survey at its original scale on lighter laptops as well as powerful workstations.

  • Explore larger surveys. Open detailed maps that would otherwise be difficult to load all at once.
  • Stay in motion. CaveViewer brings nearby parts of the map into view as you fly, keeping exploration responsive.
  • Return faster. Once a map has been prepared, CaveViewer keeps its local working data for quicker future openings.
Hardware note. CaveViewer is designed with consumer-grade hardware in mind, but exceptionally old systems can still struggle with rendering. Older Intel-based Macs, for example, may offer lower performance.

Explore the Map Library

Getting started with unfamiliar mapping software is difficult when you do not have a prepared data set. Map Library offers a catalog of standard maps that can be downloaded on demand, reopened locally, and used to check that CaveViewer is ready. Its bundled or cached catalog keeps a known selection available when a network connection is unavailable.

  • Start without your own data. Try a ready-to-use cave map as soon as you open CaveViewer.
  • Download only what you need. Choose the maps you want instead of installing a large collection you may never use.
  • Keep maps close at hand. Downloaded maps and the most recently known catalog remain useful when you have limited connectivity.
Map format note. Currently, CaveViewer supports textured OBJ maps with matching MTL files.

Record & Share Dives

A fly-through is hard to explain once the map window is closed. Capture turns what you see into a video and lets you save the route you take through the map. Everything stays in local files, ready to revisit or share through the tools your team already uses.

  • Record a dive. Save a video fly-through for a quick review or a clear visual explanation.
  • Trace a route. Save the path you take so someone else can replay that same route on the same map.
  • Slice a cave. Mark two points to pull out a smaller, standalone part of a large map—useful when you want to focus on one passage or share only the relevant area.

Performance & compatibility

Big caves, practical choices.

CaveViewer is built for the reality of photogrammetry: maps can be much larger than the memory a desktop can comfortably hold at once. It makes cautious choices first, then gives you a small set of useful controls when a particular cave or computer needs them.

Adapts to the hardware in front of it

CaveViewer uses available system and graphics memory to set a working budget. It keeps a manageable set of nearby map chunks ready instead of trying to hold an entire cave in memory. If a map’s textures exceed the graphics budget, it can select a smaller texture size before upload. You can still explore the map, though fine surface detail may look softer.

Tune the map, not your whole day

Preferences keeps the useful choices close at hand. For a dense source map, adjust the import settings before building or rebuilding its cache; for viewing, give CaveViewer only the memory and background work your desktop can comfortably spare.

Preferences → Import
Import chunk size, Max upload group size, and Cache-building worker limit help shape a new cache for the map.
Preferences → Streaming
System RAM target, GPU memory target, and Loading worker limit balance map residency and background loading while you explore.

Save the part worth returning to

Choose a start and end point in a precompiled map and save a standalone slice. A focused restriction, landmark, or passage becomes its own portable map with the geometry and textures it needs—so it can open without making you wait on the full parent cave.

Free means free

CaveViewer and its maps are free. No ads, no accounts, no subscriptions, no gimmicks, and no trackers. Just the tools and maps you need, kept in files you control.

Desktop-first, cross-platform from day one

Explore on recent Windows, macOS, or Linux systems, including Ubuntu and Fedora. Mobile support is not ready yet: maps this complex need desktop-class resources. It is a direction we are thinking about, not a promise for today.

A practical starting point

Every cave is different, but these are sensible baseline expectations for smaller maps.

  • Recent Windows, macOS, Ubuntu, or Fedora
  • 16 GB of RAM
  • At least 256 GB of free disk space

A candid note on Macs. We have had good results on Apple hardware, including early M1 systems. Older Intel-based Macs have generally been the most constrained systems in testing. CaveViewer aims to work well on consumer-grade hardware, but map size, textures, available memory, and graphics hardware still set real limits.
